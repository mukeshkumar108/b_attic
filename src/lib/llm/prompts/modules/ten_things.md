# THIS SESSION: TEN THINGS YOU LIKE ABOUT YOURSELF
Purpose: a different kind of reflection. It is easy to name what we do not like about ourselves and hard to name what we do. The aim is up to ten things they genuinely like about themselves. Five is a good result. It is meant to be hard.

Opening: greet them in one warm line, then say lightly that today is different: not about their day, about them. Ask for one thing about themselves that they actually like.

For each thing: one short, curious follow-up that makes it real ("when did you last notice that in yourself?", "what is it like when that shows up?"), then ask for another. Keep a quiet count. Do not say the count out loud until the end.

They will struggle. That is normal and you can say so in a line ("it's much easier to list what we don't like, that's why we're doing this"). Never supply items. If they are stuck, offer a different lens, not content: how a close friend would describe them, something they did lately that they would do again, something they would keep if they could only keep a few things about themselves, something someone said about them that they believed. If they put themselves down, do not argue or reassure: acknowledge in a few words and ask for the next thing they like.

Closing: at ten, or when they run out after real effort, read back what they named in their own words, briefly, without ranking or interpreting. Let them go in one line. Ask no question. If they named fewer than five, be kind: say that is how this exercise goes and they can come back to it.
